# Phase 3B — fixed-budget 20% experience replay baseline

**Decision: PASS under the preregistered engineering gate, with an important retention caveat.** Replay raised mean post-B EVAL_A pass@1 by 48.96 percentage points versus the fresh matched naive controls (47/96 versus 0/96 seed-task outcomes), while B was 25/96 versus naive 24/96 and non-GOCO regression stayed within the fixed bound. However, **none of the 24 seed-task A successes immediately after A training survived either B branch**. Replay's 47 post-B A passes were all on tasks that had *failed* pre-B. Thus the numerical gate supports improved *net post-B A performance*, **not preservation of the exact previously acquired A tasks**. It does not establish elimination of forgetting or durable task-level retention.

## Replay rule and exact compute matching

Seeds were fixed as `20260924`, `20261012`, and `20261118`; no best seed was selected. For each seed, one A adapter was trained from the same immutable Qwen2.5-Coder-3B-Instruct revision. Naive and replay B branches each reloaded that *same seed-matched A adapter* with a fresh optimizer and scheduler. Both branches used the same prompt, quantization/LoRA recipe, final-epoch checkpoint rule, greedy decoding, compiler, tests, and **60 exposure slots per epoch for three epochs: 180 micro examples and 24 optimizer steps**. Naive used 60 B examples/epoch. Replay deterministically replaced 12 B positions/epoch with six previously verified A examples from each A subskill, leaving 48 B + 12 A (80%/20%). Six B positions per B subskill were replaced. Thirty-six unique A IDs appeared across each seed's three replay epochs. The two branches shared B ordering except for those replacements. All nine training records passed step, lineage, base-weight, and frozen-schedule hash checks. No extra replay compute was added.

Base non-GOCO regression was 47/64. A immediately after A was 25% on every seed, all from the pair-product-gap archetype within array reduction; numeric iteration was 0/16. Replay B acquisition was 9/32, 8/32, 8/32, also entirely field processing; string transformation remained 0/16 for all B checkpoints. Thus mean B was 25.00% naive and 26.04% replay, a replay/naive ratio of 104.17%. This is no evidence of broad B mastery.

The **net** absolute A forgetting, computed as A-only rate minus post-B rate, was 25 points in every naive seed and -21.875, -25, -25 points under replay; a negative number here is net score gain, not retention of previously correct tasks. Post-B/A-only rate ratios were 0% naive and 187.5%, 200%, 200% replay. Crucially, paired transitions from the A-only checkpoint were: naive 24 pass→fail and 72 fail→fail over 96 seed-task instances; replay also had **24 pass→fail**, plus 47 fail→pass and 25 fail→fail. Replay preserved **0/24** original A successes. Its new A successes were principally divisor-pair-sum numeric tasks (8 per seed) and extrema-spread array tasks (7/8/8); the original pair-product-gap successes disappeared in all seeds. The apparent negative forgetting reflects a template/subskill shift, not stable individual-task retention.

## Gate, uncertainty, failures, and validity

The frozen gate required (1) at least a 20-point mean post-B A advantage, (2) replay B at least 75% of a positive naive mean B, and (3) no replay non-GOCO score more than nine of 64 items below *either* seed-matched naive or base. Observed values were **+48.96 points**, **104.17%**, and a worst replay deficit of **2 items** (seed two versus naive). All three components pass, so the formal decision is **PASS**. A 10,000-draw paired task-cluster bootstrap, resampling 32 task IDs while keeping their three seed observations together, gives a descriptive 95% interval of **31.25–65.625 points** for the post-B A difference. This interval is not a substitute for replication on new task families; archetype variants are correlated, and seeds are not 96 independent task replicates.

The failure taxonomy reinforces the narrowness: across three seeds, A-only EVAL_A had 24 successes, 48 hidden-test-semantic failures, 24 syntax failures. Naive post-B EVAL_A had zero successes, with 57 semantic, 23 runtime, 8 hidden-test-semantic, and 8 syntax failures. Replay post-B EVAL_A had 47 successes, 34 semantic, 14 hidden-test-semantic, and one syntax failure. On EVAL_B, naive had 24 successes, 37 semantic, 23 syntax, and 12 hidden-test-semantic failures; replay had 25 successes, 34 semantic, 28 syntax, and 9 hidden-test-semantic failures. The full per-seed/subskill taxonomy and 1,280 compact task outcomes are in `analysis.json` and `task_outcomes.json`.

There were **no post-freeze methodological deviations**: no seed, data, mix, hyperparameter, prompt, checkpoint-selection, scoring, or gate changes, and no model-evaluation retry or manual repair. Post-freeze dispatch, analysis, and compact-publication scripts only executed and audited the frozen method. Two rejected structural-audit construction attempts preceded the preregistration. Phase 3B's mutable adapters, generations, and intermediate records remained in gitignored `.runtime/phase3b/`, not LFS; no finalized adapter was copied to research storage because hashes plus retained local runtime suffice for this bounded comparison. The GOCO product checkout and `.git` internals were not modified; no GC/prune/history rewrite was run.

**Supported:** Under this fixed 20% replay schedule and matched 24-step QLoRA control, replay substantially improved net post-B A pass@1 on these fresh, narrow GOCO tasks without reducing measured B acquisition or causing severe regression on the frozen 64-item control. **Not supported:** preservation of any originally passed A task, elimination of forgetting, general superiority or optimality of replay, robust acquisition of both A/B subskills, autonomous/self-learning, or lifelong continual learning. The preregistered numerical PASS and the task-level retention failure must be reported together.

**Recommended next experiment (not started):** independently replicate with broader, more structurally distant A/B tasks; require a meaningful pre-B A acquisition threshold across *both* A subskills; preregister preservation of previously passed A task IDs as a co-primary outcome alongside aggregate post-B A score. Keep a matched fixed-budget naive control and sealed final-paper holdout. **STOP** here for independent review; do not change replay percentage or test another continual-learning method automatically.
