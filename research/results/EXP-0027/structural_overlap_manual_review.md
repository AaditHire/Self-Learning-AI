# Phase 3A pre-training structural review

Reviewed 2026-09-23, before any Phase 3A gradient run. The complete machine-readable nearest-neighbor records and verified reference results are in `data_validation.json`. The initial construction audit (`data_validation_attempt_01.json`) found eight EVAL_A odd-cube references above 0.98 normalized-code similarity to a consumed evaluation reference. Their reference implementation was restructured before freezing. The final audit has no such flags.

Both distributions have 60 training examples (30 per subskill) and 32 evaluation tasks (16 per subskill), five semantic cases per item. A requires numeric loops/recurrences and array computation; B requires string transformations and delimited-field processing. They share GOCO `IMPORT`, typed variables, `INPUT`, and `DISPLAYNL`, while their central operations and data types differ. Every target/reference passes all five compiler-executed cases. The sealed holdout was not opened.

Within A, all 32 evaluation references have normalized-code similarity at least 0.85 to some training reference; none reach 0.98. Within B, 16/32 do. These similarities mostly reflect the language's short input/declaration/display boilerplate. The A evaluation algorithms (odd-cube filtering, offset product with length subtraction, negative-entry square sum, and pairwise array products) differ in computation and operation combination from A training algorithms (divisible square sum, affine recurrence, threshold-weighted array sum, indexed difference). The B evaluation algorithms (trim/lower/suffix, reverse/replace, case-joined fields, dual-field containment) differ from B training (upper/reverse/suffix, replace/lower, swapped/tagged fields, field counts). No evaluation prompt, algorithmic-structure label, lineage, structural-signature label, or semantic-operation combination exactly matches its training split.

Eight A and 24 B evaluation tasks match a training AST-proxy signature. The proxy records only broad keywords/operators and is insensitive to operand order, constants, and many library calls. I checked all matching archetypes against their nearest training archetypes: A negative-square filtering is not threshold-weighted summation; B trim/lower/suffix is not upper/reverse/suffix; B reverse/replace is not replace/lower; B case-joined fields is not field swap/tag. No identical solution strategy is present despite the proxy collisions. This remains a limitation: the suites test transfer among compact GOCO patterns, not distant algorithmic generalization.

No Phase 3A training or evaluation prompt exactly reuses a consumed Phase 1R/1S/1T/2A/2B evaluation prompt; there are zero prior-prompt flags at 0.70. Eight A and 24 B evaluation references have a prior normalized-code neighbor at or above 0.90, again driven in part by concise GOCO boilerplate and common library operations. None reaches 0.98, and the prompts and requested algorithms differ. The Phase 2B non-GOCO regression suite is deliberately reused without training exposure, so it remains the same longitudinal control.

No performance-conditioned selection was used; all construction changes were based on reference validity and structural separation before training.
